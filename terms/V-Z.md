### VA Disability Compensation
*Source: **irs_vita***

VA disability compensation is a monetary benefit paid to veterans who are disabled because of injury or disease incurred or aggravated during active military service.

### Vacation home
*Source: **turbotax***

Special tax rules apply if you rent out a vacation home, and the rules differ depending on how much you use the home personally. While all rental income is to be reported, the deductibility of expenses can be limited if you engage in "too much" personal use—generally defined as using the home for more than 14 days during the year or more than 10% of the number of days it is rented for fair market rent.

### Value-added tax (VAT)**
*Source: **taxpolicycenter***

A form of consumption tax collected from businesses on the value firms add to a product at each stage of production (instead of being collected wholly from sales to final consumers, as in a retail sales tax). VATs are almost universal outside the US.

### Vertical equity
*Source: **irs_student***

The concept that people in different income groups should pay different rates of taxes or different percentages of their incomes as taxes. "Unequals should be taxed unequally."

**Additional context:**

> A concept where people of different income levels should pay different tax rates or percentages of their incomes as taxes. “Unequals should be tax unequally.”

---
### Vertical equity**
*Source: **taxpolicycenter***

The concept that net tax burden should be higher on people with higher levels of well-being. The degree to which the tax burden should increase for people with increased well-being is a value judgment (See also Horizontal equity.)

### Vested benefits
*Source: **turbotax***

Benefits in a company retirement plan that are yours to keep if you leave the job. Your own contributions to a 401(k) are immediately 100% vested. But employer contributions on your behalf can be vested gradually over a period of time, as a way to encourage you to stay with the employer. If you quit a job when just 50% of your benefits are vested, for example, you would forfeit half of the amount the employer has set aside for you.

### Voluntary compliance
*Source: **irs_student***

A system of compliance that relies on individual citizens to report their income freely and voluntarily, calculate their tax liability correctly, and file a tax return on time.

**Additional context:**

> A concept where individuals freely and voluntarily report their income, calculate their tax liability, and file their taxes on time. The U.S. tax system relies on voluntary compliance.

### Voluntary withholding
*Source: **turbotax***

You can ask the Social Security Administration to withhold federal income taxes from your Social Security benefits. This could make sense if withholding allows you to avoid making quarterly estimated tax payments. To request voluntary withholding, file form W-4V with Social Security. You can also ask a retirement plan sponsor to withhold from payouts from IRA or other retirement distributions. ### W

### Wage base
*Source: **turbotax***

The level of earnings to which the full Social Security tax applies. For 2026, the full 15.30% tax (the combined rate paid by employers and employees) applies to the first $184,500 of wages or self-employment income, and the 2.9% Medicare portion applies to all income over that level. For 2025, the Social Security wage limit was $176,100. (Employees pay part of the tax—7.65% up to the wage base limit and 1.45% after that—and their employers pay the other half. Self-employed taxpayers have to pay both halves.)

---
### Wage Garnishment
*Source: **taxdefense***

Money legally withheld from a person’s wages to satisfy a debt, such as delinquent child support payments, defaulted student loans, or unpaid taxes.

---
### Wages
*Source: **irs_student***

Compensation received by employees for services performed. Usually, wages are computed by multiplying an hourly pay rate by the number of hours worked.

**Additional context:**

> Wages are compensation received by employees for services performed.

---
### Wash sale
*Source: **irs_vita***

A wash sale occurs when a taxpayer sells or otherwise disposes of stock or securities (including a contract or option to acquire or sell stock or securities) at a loss and, within 30 days before or after the sale or disposition, the taxpayer buys, acquires, or enters into a contract or option to acquire substantially identical stock or securities.

**Additional context:**

> The sale of stocks, bonds or mutual fund shares for a loss when, within 30 days before or after that sale, you buy the same or substantially identical securities. The law forbids the deduction of the loss.

### Withholding
*Source: **turbotax***

Money withheld from a person’s paycheck to help cover their tax liability, such as Social Security, Medicare, and federal income tax.

**Additional context:**

> The amount held back from your wages each payday to pay your income taxes for the year. The amount withheld is based on the size of your salary and the W-4 form you file with your employer.

---
### Withholding ("pay-as-you-earn" taxation)
*Source: **irs_student***

Money, for example, that employers withhold from employees paychecks. This money is deposited for the government. (It will be credited against the employees' tax liability when they file their returns.) Employers withhold money for federal income taxes, Social Security taxes and state and local income taxes in some states and localities.

---
### Withholding Allowance
*Source: **irs_vita***

Claimed by an employee on Form W-4. An employer uses the number of allowances claimed, together with income earned and marital status, to determine how much income tax to withhold from wages.

**Additional context:**

> An exemption previously claimed on Form W-4 that reduced the amount of income tax an employer deducted from an employee’s paycheck. In 2020, however, the allowances section was eliminated.

---
### Withholding Tax
*Source: **irs_vita***

Income tax is withheld from the pay of most employees. Income tax may also be withheld from gambling winnings, pensions/annuities, unemployment compensation, and certain federal payments, such as social security. In some cases income tax may be withheld on other types of income such as interest or dividend income.

**Additional context:**

> The amount of income tax that is automatically deducted from an employee’s wages or a taxpayer’s income to meet tax obligations throughout the year.

### Worldwide Income
*Source: **irs_vita***

U.S. citizens and U.S. resident aliens are required to report worldwide income on a U.S. tax return regardless of where they live and even if the income is taxed by the country in which it was earned. Filing requirements are the same as for U.S. citizens and U.S. resident aliens living in the United States and apply whether income is from within or outside the U.S.

**Additional context:**

> S. citizens and U.S. resident aliens are required to report worldwide income on a U.S. tax return regardless of where they live and even if the income is taxed by the country in which it was earned.
